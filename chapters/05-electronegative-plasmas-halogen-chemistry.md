# Chapter 5: Electronegative Plasmas & Halogen Dissociation Chemistry

## 5.1 Electron Attachment & Negative Ion Trapping
Unlike electropositive argon plasmas, semiconductor etch chemistries utilize halogen gases ($\text{Cl}_2$, $\text{F}_2$, $\text{SF}_6$, $\text{HBr}$, $\text{C}_4\text{F}_8$) that greedily capture low-energy electrons via dissociative attachment:

$$e^- + \text{Cl}_2 \to \text{Cl}^- + \text{Cl}$$

$$e^- + \text{SF}_6 \to \text{SF}_5^- + \text{F}$$

This creates dense populations of negative ions ($\text{Cl}^-$, $\text{F}^-$). Because the plasma sheath potential is positive relative to the walls, negative ions cannot escape to the boundaries; they remain electrostatically trapped in the core plasma.

## 5.2 Electronegativity ($\\alpha = n_- / n_e$)
In halogen discharges, the electronegativity $\alpha$ can reach values of $10\text{ to } 100$. As free electrons are depleted:
- Core plasma electrical conductivity drops.
- A secondary internal presheath forms, dividing the discharge into an electronegative core and an electropositive halo.
- Instabilities and low-frequency oscillations ($10\text{–}100\text{ kHz}$) can manifest, requiring precision gas balancing with argon or helium diluents.
