# Chapter 4: Inductively Coupled Plasmas (ICP) & High-Density Sources

## 4.1 Electromagnetic Induction & Power Coupling
Inductively Coupled Plasma (ICP) reactors apply RF power to an external planar or helical copper coil separated from the vacuum chamber by a dielectric alumina or quartz window.
- The RF current through the coil generates a time-varying magnetic field $\mathbf{B}(t)$, which in turn induces an azimuthal RF electric field $\mathbf{E}_\theta(t)$ via Faraday's Law:

$$\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t}$$

## 4.2 The E-to-H Mode Transition
1. **E-Mode (Capacitive Mode):** At low RF power, the electric field between coil turns drives weak capacitive discharge ($n_e \sim 10^9\text{–}10^{10}\text{ cm}^{-3}$).
2. **H-Mode (Inductive Mode):** Once coil current exceeds a threshold, inductive power transfer dominates, causing density to surge by orders of magnitude to **$n_e \sim 10^{11}\text{–}10^{12}\text{ cm}^{-3}$**.

## 4.3 High-Density Processing Advantages
- Low pressure operation ($1\text{–}10\text{ mTorr}$), reducing gas scattering and maximizing ion directionality.
- Independent substrate bias applied via a separate RF generator on the electrostatic chuck.
