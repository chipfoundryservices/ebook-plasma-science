# Chapter 7: In-Situ Plasma Diagnostics & Optical Emission Spectroscopy

## 7.1 Optical Emission Spectroscopy (OES)
As excited atoms and radicals relax to lower electronic states, they emit discrete photons:

$$\Delta E = h \nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$$

By collecting light through a quartz viewport with a fiber-optic spectrometer ($200\text{–}900\text{ nm}$), fabs detect chemical species without perturbing the vacuum.
- **Actinometry:** Adding a trace amount of noble gas (e.g., $5\%\\text{ Ar}$ at $750.4\text{ nm}$) normalizes emission intensity, converting raw optical signals into absolute radical concentrations ($[\text{F}]$, $[\text{Cl}]$).

## 7.2 In-Situ Endpoint Detection
When an etching film (e.g., $\text{Si}_3\text{N}_4$) is cleared and the underlying layer (e.g., $\text{SiO}_2$) is exposed:
- Nitrogen radical emission ($\text{CN}$ or $\text{N}_2$ lines at $387\text{ nm}$) abruptly drops.
- Reaction byproduct emission ($\text{SiF}_2$ at $395\text{ nm}$) surges.
Real-time multivariate statistical algorithms detect this transition within $0.1\text{ seconds}$, terminating RF power automatically.
